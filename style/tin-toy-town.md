# Tin Toy Town: style guide

A portable visual style for heists, chases, cities and business stories, in video, thumbnails and Shorts. It works in any project: swap the toys, keep the rules.

**In one line:** the story is played out on a tabletop with vintage lithographed tin toys and plain wooden peg figures with no faces, shot like a small, warm stop-motion set.

**Why it is face-free:** peg dolls are turned wood with a plain round head. A character is a peg with one tiny hat and a coloured band. Tin cars, banks, ships and trains are rigid objects, which video models move convincingly without changing their shape.

**Where it comes from:** 1920s to 1950s lithographed tin toys, German turned-wood toy villages (Erzgebirge), and tabletop miniature and stop-motion photography. *References from memory.*

---

## 1. Core rules

1. **Everything is a toy on a table.** Real light, shallow depth of field, a plain cream backdrop.
2. **People are wooden pegs.** Plain round head, no painted face, one hat, one coloured band.
3. **Buildings and vehicles are printed tin.** Lithographed windows and details, slightly worn paint.
4. **Toy scale is honest.** A car is bigger than a peg; a bank is bigger than a car.
5. **The camera is at toy eye level** or slightly above, never a drone shot.
6. **One accent:** the case study's hatband, or the getaway car.
7. **Victims are never toys.** Deaths are shown by the empty table or a fallen hat, told straight in the voice.

## 2. Palette

| Role | Share | Default (Dream Job) |
|---|---|---|
| Backdrop and table | 30 to 50% | cream `#EFE3C8` |
| Tin colours | 30 to 45% | teal `#2F7C78`, mustard `#D9A23A` |
| Wood | 5 to 15% | natural pale wood |
| Ink | 5 to 15% | `#24211D` hats, wheels, lines |
| Accent | under 5% | red `#C23A2B` |

## 3. Cast

| Character | Peg |
|---|---|
| Case study | Peg with his hat and a red band |
| Crew | Pegs with the same hat, ink band |
| Police | Pegs with a peaked cap |
| Public | Plain pegs, no hat |
| Rival gang | Pegs with a different hat in a tin colour |

## 4. Motion

- **In MiniMax: excellent.** Wind-up cars driving off, tin trains, ships rocking, doors opening, pegs sliding or hopping, a toy safe door swinging. Prompt like stop-motion: "the tin car drives slowly out of frame to the right, slight stop-motion jitter, camera static".
- Remotion for text, cuts, stamps and counters.
- QA: pegs growing faces or arms, toys changing paint, extra wheels.

## 5. Prompt template

```
Vintage 1950s tin toy town diorama on a tabletop. [Tin toys: building, vehicle] and small turned wooden peg-doll figures with plain round heads and no faces, [the case study: tiny hat and red band], [action]. Printed tin textures, slightly worn paint, soft warm light, shallow depth of field, cream backdrop. Palette: cream #EFE3C8, mustard #D9A23A, teal #2F7C78, ink #24211D, red #C23A2B. No text, no letters, no painted faces.
```

## 6. Building assets

1. One reference image per recurring toy (the getaway car, the bank, his peg) and reuse it as an image reference for later shots.
2. Credit budget: about 3 references per episode plus 1 frame per shot.

## 7. When to use it

**Strong for:** heists, chases, bank robbers, gangsters' empires, business and finance explainers, kids-safe retellings of tough history. Very reusable across channels.

**Weak for:** grief and close personal moments; switch to Turned Away or Cut Paper Noir there.

## 8. Checklist

- [ ] No painted faces on any peg.
- [ ] Toys match their reference images.
- [ ] Toy-level camera, shallow focus.
- [ ] One accent.
- [ ] No text in the art; no victim shown as a toy.
