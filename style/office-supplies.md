# Office Supply Cast: style guide

A portable visual style for video, thumbnails, covers and Shorts. It works in any project: swap the palette and the props, keep the rules.

**In one line:** the cast is the stationery on the training-room desk: a stapler is the boss, paper clips are the staff, a rubber stamp is the law.

**The twist:** The company training film made literal: the office itself plays the story.

**Why it is face-free:** Stationery has no face. Each character is a real object with a fixed shape, and objects stay consistent in image and video models.

**Where it comes from:** Tabletop stop-motion and object animation (Norman McLaren's object films at the National Film Board of Canada, 1940s to 1950s), and mid-century office-supply advertising. *References from memory.* Take the technique, never copy a specific design.

---

## 1. Core rules

1. Every character is one office object, fixed for the series.
2. Status is the object's weight: the boss is the heaviest thing on the desk.
3. The case study wears one tiny hat (fedora, tricorn) on his object.
4. The desk is the world: blotter as ground, pencils as columns or bars, ledgers as buildings.
5. Warm stop-motion light, shallow focus.
6. One accent: the red rubber stamp, which only ever means the law or a verdict.
7. Deaths: the object is put away in a drawer, which closes. Told straight.

## 2. Palette

| Role | Share | Default (Dream Job) |
|---|---|---|
| Ground | 40 to 60% | cream `#EFE3C8` or the set colour |
| Main colours | 20 to 35% | mustard `#D9A23A`, teal `#2F7C78` |
| Ink | 10 to 20% | `#24211D` |
| Accent | under 5% | red `#C23A2B`, once per frame |

**Greyscale check:** red and teal are the same grey in the default palette, so the accent never sits on teal. Other channels keep the structure: one ground, two mid-tones, an ink, one hot accent.

## 3. Cast

Boss: stapler or hole punch with his hat. Staff: bent paper-clip figures. Accountant: a calculator. Police and courts: rubber stamps and a gavel-shaped stamp handle. Money: real coins and banknotes. Prison: a pencil cup.

Make one reference sheet per recurring character (front, side, back, on a plain background) and use it as the image reference for every later shot.

## 4. Composition and type

- One or two characters per frame, one clear action.
- Leave one clean area for live text in thumbnails and title cards.
- All text, stamps and the career file are added in Remotion, never generated.

## 5. Motion

**In MiniMax:** Excellent. Rigid objects slide, hop and topple well; the stapler 'chomping', paper clips shuffling, a stamp coming down. One action per clip.

General MiniMax rules: one motion per clip, describe camera and motion only, no text in the frame, and QA the contact sheet for faces appearing, extra limbs and duplicated objects. Remotion handles cuts, text, stamps and counters.

## 6. Prompt template

```
Tabletop scene on a cream office desk blotter, shot like warm stop-motion. The characters are office supplies standing like people: [boss object with tiny hat], [staff objects], [law object] [action]. Soft warm light, shallow depth of field. Palette: cream #EFE3C8, mustard #D9A23A, teal #2F7C78, ink #24211D, red #C23A2B. No faces, no eyes, no text.
```

## 7. When to use it

**Strong for:** Dream Job above all (it is the training room), business and workplace channels, satire, finance explainers.

**Weak for:** Pirates and the Wild West (the office frame needs a reason; keep the desk and add period props).

## 8. Checklist

- [ ] No face, eyes or mouth anywhere.
- [ ] Each character matches his reference sheet.
- [ ] One accent, not on teal.
- [ ] No text in the art.
- [ ] Deaths and victims told straight, never as a gag.
- [ ] Reads at 168 px wide (thumbnail size).
