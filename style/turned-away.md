# Turned Away: style guide

A portable visual style for drama, biography and quiet moments, in video, thumbnails and book interiors. It works in any project: swap the palette, keep the rules.

**In one line:** flat mid-century painted scenes where the people are always seen from behind, from below the hat brim, or cropped at the shoulders, so the face is never in frame.

**Why it is face-free:** the camera simply never sees a face. Characters are recognised by their back: hat, coat, shoulders, posture. A back is far easier for a model to repeat than a face, and it reads as lonely and serious, which suits the straight-told lessons.

**Where it comes from:** Edward Hopper's quiet compositions, Caspar David Friedrich's figures seen from behind (the *Rückenfigur*), and flat 1950s magazine and animation background painting (Amid Amidi, *Cartoon Modern*, Chronicle Books, 2006). *References from memory.*

---

## 1. Core rules

1. **The face is never in frame.** From behind, or cropped at the neck, or from far away under a hat brim. If a shot needs the face, change the angle.
2. **The back is the character.** Each one has a fixed hat, coat colour and build.
3. **Flat colour planes, few details.** Rooms are walls, windows and long hard sunlight shapes.
4. **The character looks at the story.** He stands before what matters: a window over his city, a courtroom, a prison gate, the sea.
5. **Stillness.** Little action; the weight comes from waiting and looking.
6. **One accent**, often on the character himself (a tie, a band) or on what he looks at.

## 2. Palette

| Role | Share | Default (Dream Job) |
|---|---|---|
| Walls and light | 30 to 50% | cream `#EFE3C8`, mustard `#D9A23A` sunlight shapes |
| Outside and shade | 20 to 35% | teal `#2F7C78` |
| Figure and lines | 15 to 30% | ink `#24211D` |
| Accent | under 3% | red `#C23A2B` |

## 3. Shot list

| Shot | Use |
|---|---|
| At the window, from behind | Ambition, owning the city, waiting |
| In a doorway, from behind | Hiring, arriving, leaving |
| Over the shoulder at a desk | Work, money, a letter |
| Walking away down a corridor | Disciplinary action, prison |
| Seated, back to camera, facing a judge's bench | Trial |
| Empty room, his hat on the chair | Exit interview, told straight |

## 4. Motion

- **In MiniMax: very good, with one risk.** Slow light shifts, curtains moving, smoke from a cigarette, a slow walk away, a push-in on his back. Risk: video models like to turn the figure to face the camera. Always prompt "he does not turn around, his face is never visible" and cut any clip where he turns.
- Remotion for text, cuts and stamps.

## 5. Prompt template

```
Flat mid-century painted illustration in the spirit of 1950s magazine art and quiet Edward Hopper compositions. A man in [hat and coat] seen strictly from behind, [action] [location], his face never visible. Long hard light shapes, simple flat colour planes, no detail. Palette: cream #EFE3C8, mustard #D9A23A, teal #2F7C78, ink #24211D, one small red #C23A2B [accent]. No text, no visible face.
```

## 6. When to use it

**Strong for:** biography, drama, the exit interview, reflection, trials, prison, any channel that tells serious life stories.

**Weak for:** action, comedy, explaining. Pair with Isotype Ledger or Evidence Table.

## 7. Checklist

- [ ] No face anywhere, including reflections in windows and mirrors.
- [ ] The character's hat and coat match his reference.
- [ ] Flat planes, long light shapes.
- [ ] One accent.
- [ ] No text in the art.
