# Origami: style guide

A portable visual style for video, thumbnails, covers and Shorts. It works in any project: swap the palette and the props, keep the rules.

**In one line:** the whole world is folded paper: ships, cities, waves and people folded from single sheets, with plain triangular heads.

**The twist:** Everything can unfold. A scene can fold itself up into the next, and a career can be unfolded back to a flat sheet.

**Why it is face-free:** Folded figures have plain geometric heads. A character is a paper colour plus one folded hat. Crisp geometric folds are easy to repeat.

**Where it comes from:** Japanese and Western paper folding, mid-century paper sculpture in advertising, and paper-craft stop-motion. *References from memory.* Take the technique, never copy a specific design.

---

## 1. Core rules

1. Everything is folded, never cut and glued (that is Cut Paper Noir).
2. Heads are plain folded triangles or diamonds. No drawn faces.
3. Each character has one paper colour and one folded hat.
4. Visible creases and soft side light show the paper.
5. Scenes are small dioramas on a cream paper ground.
6. One accent: a red paper sash, flag or seal.
7. Death: the figure is gently unfolded flat. Told straight.

## 2. Palette

| Role | Share | Default (Dream Job) |
|---|---|---|
| Ground | 40 to 60% | cream `#EFE3C8` or the set colour |
| Main colours | 20 to 35% | mustard `#D9A23A`, teal `#2F7C78` |
| Ink | 10 to 20% | `#24211D` |
| Accent | under 5% | red `#C23A2B`, once per frame |

**Greyscale check:** red and teal are the same grey in the default palette, so the accent never sits on teal. Other channels keep the structure: one ground, two mid-tones, an ink, one hot accent.

## 3. Cast

Case study: his colour plus folded hat (tricorn, fedora). Crew: same hat, plain paper. Navy or police: ink-black paper with a peaked fold. Public: cream paper.

Make one reference sheet per recurring character (front, side, back, on a plain background) and use it as the image reference for every later shot.

## 4. Composition and type

- One or two characters per frame, one clear action.
- Leave one clean area for live text in thumbnails and title cards.
- All text, stamps and the career file are added in Remotion, never generated.

## 5. Motion

**In MiniMax:** Good. Paper waves rocking, a boat sailing, paper flags flapping, slow pans. Unfolding transitions are hard to control in MiniMax; do those in Remotion as crossfades or as one-off tests.

General MiniMax rules: one motion per clip, describe camera and motion only, no text in the frame, and QA the contact sheet for faces appearing, extra limbs and duplicated objects. Remotion handles cuts, text, stamps and counters.

## 6. Prompt template

```
Origami diorama: [scene] folded from [colour] paper, small folded-paper figures with plain triangular heads and no faces, [case study with folded hat and red sash] [action]. Crisp folds, visible creases, soft side light, cream paper background. Palette: cream #EFE3C8, mustard #D9A23A, teal #2F7C78, ink #24211D, red #C23A2B. No faces, no text.
```

## 7. When to use it

**Strong for:** Pirates, ships, journeys, Asian history (Ching Shih), kids and family channels, gentle explainers.

**Weak for:** Gritty crime and violence; paper reads soft. Pair with Neon Night or Cut Paper Noir.

## 8. Checklist

- [ ] No face, eyes or mouth anywhere.
- [ ] Each character matches his reference sheet.
- [ ] One accent, not on teal.
- [ ] No text in the art.
- [ ] Deaths and victims told straight, never as a gag.
- [ ] Reads at 168 px wide (thumbnail size).
